Kaigo Drill

Study app for the Long-Term Care Specified Skilled Worker (Tokutei Ginou / SSW) Skill Evaluation Test

  • iPhoneAvailable
  • AndroidComing soon

The Android version is being prepared for release on Google Play (planned for December 2026). We will announce the release on the Haven Atelier Facebook page.

App version: 1.5.0 (released November 9, 2026)

Kaigo Drill helps you prepare for the Long-Term Care Specified Skilled Worker (Tokutei Ginou / SSW) Skill Evaluation Test.

The real exam is taken in the language of the country where you sit it. This app works the same way: questions and answer choices appear in your own language, while the Japanese used on the job is shown alongside, with furigana on every kanji.

App Store (iPhone)Android: coming soon

Features

  • Practice by subject (basics of care, mind and body, communication, daily-living support)
  • Practice for the practical part: 27 questions where you look at a picture and judge the right care step
  • A mock exam in the same format as the real one: 45 questions, 60 minutes, and you can pause it
  • Questions you get wrong come back automatically for review
  • Japanese alongside: none, key terms only, or the full text
  • Works offline
  • A daily study reminder at a time you choose

Languages

Indonesian, Burmese, Nepali, English and Japanese

About the questions

Questions and explanations are written from public Japanese government sources: the Long-Term Care Insurance Act, the Act on the Prevention of Elder Abuse, and Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare guidance on infection control in care settings, workplace back-pain prevention, and care prevention. The source is shown with each question.

About the exam

Test names
The care skills evaluation test and the care Japanese-language evaluation test (full names: the Long-Term Care Specified Skilled Worker Evaluation Test (Skills) and (Japanese))Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”Test guidelines (skills)Test guidelines (Japanese)
Who sets the test
The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare. The tests are delivered and run by an organisation that the ministry subsidises.Test guidelines (skills)
Format
CBT (you answer on a computer at a test centre). As a rule, held every month.Test guidelines (skills)Test guidelines (Japanese)
Where you can take it
In Japan and in other countries. Venues and dates are listed on a dedicated website, reached from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare page.Test guidelines (skills)Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”
Test language
The care skills evaluation test is in the language of the country where it is held. The care Japanese-language evaluation test is in Japanese.Test guidelines (skills)Test guidelines (Japanese)
Care skills evaluation test
45 questions, 60 minutes: 40 written questions (basics of care work 10, mind and body 6, communication 4, daily-living support skills 20) and 5 practical questions, in which you look at photos and similar material and judge the correct care steps and the like.Test guidelines (skills)Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”
Care Japanese-language evaluation test
15 questions, 30 minutes (care vocabulary 5, care conversation and speaking to the people you care for 5, care documents 5)Test guidelines (Japanese)Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”
Pass mark
60% or more of the total score (skills), 73% or more (Japanese), from 1 April 2026. The test guidelines say the pass mark is set with adjustments for question difficulty and other factors.Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”Test guidelines (skills)
Test fee
About 2,000 yen for each test (for tests from 1 April 2026)Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Notice of revised test fees”
Who can take it
People aged 17 or over (18 or over for Indonesian nationals). To take it in Japan, you need a status of residence.Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”
Booking and retaking
You can book up to 60 days ahead. After you take a test, you cannot take the next one for 45 days.Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”
Results
Shown on screen at the end of the test. Within about 5 business days, you can get your score report from the dedicated website.Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”
Sample questions
Sample questions (PDF) for both tests are on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare page.Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: “Accepting Specified Skilled Workers in the Care Field”

Frequently asked questions

What can I study with this app?

  • The 4 written subjects of the care skills evaluation test (240 questions)
  • Practice for the practical part, judging from pictures (27 questions)
  • A mock exam in the same format as the real one (45 questions, 60 minutes)
  • Care vocabulary (48 words)
  • Review of the questions you got wrong, and the number of days left until your exam

Does it cost anything?

There is no charge to download it or to use it, and there is nothing to buy inside the app. It is run on ads.

Which devices can I use it on?

It is an iPhone app (iOS 16.4 or later). The Android version is being prepared for release on Google Play (planned for December 2026).

Which languages can I study in?

The app shows Japanese side by side with one of Indonesian, Burmese, Nepali or English. You choose the language when you first open the app, and you can change it at any time in Settings. For the Japanese shown with the questions, you can choose “None”, “Key terms” or “Full text” under “Japanese alongside” in Settings.

Can I use it without an internet connection?

Yes. The questions, explanations and vocabulary are stored in the app, so you can answer questions without a connection.

What are the questions based on?

Following the subject areas set in the test guidelines, the questions are based on laws and on materials published by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (the Long-Term Care Insurance Act, the Act on the Prevention of Elder Abuse, the “Zero Physical Restraint” handbook, the infection control guide, the guidelines on preventing back pain, the care prevention manual, and study texts produced under a Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare project). Every question shows the source it is based on.

Do I need to create an account?

No. Your study records are saved on your iPhone and are not sent to the operator. The Progress card reaches only the person you choose, and only when you send it yourself.
